What's in it

Ready runs in the simulator today Coming written, not run yet Planned on the roadmap

Glass home screen Ready

Your games on a shelf, a quick-settings drawer you pull down like on a phone, and a profile with your play time.

A boot you can watch Ready

The cartjam mark and a progress bar from the first seconds, with a line about what's happening, until the home screen is up.

One gamepad, no setup Ready

Buttons and sticks act as one controller, so games and emulators work without mapping. FN + Start leaves any game.

PSP games and homebrew Ready

PPSSPP is built in, and the install page offers free games straight from their authors.

Power modes Ready

Saver, Balanced or Performance from the drawer, and battery states you can read at a glance.

Updates with a safety net Coming

Two copies of the system. An update installs to the spare one, and the console goes back to the old one if the new one doesn't start.

A store for makers Planned

Share or sell games you made to people who own the same handheld.

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Planned

Joining networks and pairing controllers from the drawer, on consoles that have them.

Three steps to put it on your console

There's no release yet. The install page lists it as soon as there is one.

Open the install page

  1. 1

    Pick your console and a release on the install page.

  2. 2

    Write it to a microSD card with Etcher, or with one command on macOS or Linux.

  3. 3

    Put the card in the first slot and switch on. The first boot makes a GAMES drive, then shows the home screen.

Made a game? Play it on a handheld.

cartjam's home screen is itself written in LÖVE, the Lua game framework, and every cartjam console runs LÖVE out of the box. That's where making games for it starts.

Only games you're allowed to share

cartjam ships emulators, never games or BIOS files you don't own, and never links to copies of commercial games. The free games on the install page come from their authors' own pages, with their licenses and credits.
